# FundMore Brand Skill (Full)

FundMore is Canada's AI-powered mortgage and lending infrastructure company. Everything here
reflects the current brand canon. Read this file for visual identity, voice rules, messaging
framework, competitive positioning, and the Pre-Flight Checklist.

## 04. Voice and Tone

### Core Voice Description
FundMore's voice is the informed infrastructure partner who speaks at the same level as
the lender's risk team. Precise, evidence-led, compliance-aware. We speak to practitioners
and executives, not consumers.

### Voice Pillars
**Compliance-confident.** Lenders operate in OSFI's B-20 environment. FundMore makes that
easier, not more complicated. Speak to audit trails, explainability, and defensible decisions.

**Outcome-first.** Lead with what the lender gains: faster decisions, lower cost-to-close,
reduced manual error. Never open with feature names.

**Numbers-led.** Quantify where possible. "Faster" is weak. "Days to hours" is strong.

**Canadian and precise.** Reference OSFI, B-20, FINTRAC, CMHC where accurate. Never
use Canadian regulatory terms as decoration -- only when they apply.

### Tone by Context

| Channel | Tone |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn / thought-leadership | Authoritative, question-driving. Practitioner peer, not vendor. |
| Regulation posts | Neutral explainer first, then connection to FundMore without overclaiming. |
| Product spotlight | Outcome-first, one proof point, specific buyer language. |
| Enterprise / C-suite | ROI and compliance framing, concise, no feature parade. |

### Write Like This
- "Your underwriters are spending time on decisions the data already made."
- "B-20 audits don't forgive 'we've always done it that way.'"
- "Explainability isn't a nice-to-have. It's what OSFI expects."
- "Every manual touch is a cost. Every inconsistency is a risk."

### Never Write Like This
- "FundMore's cutting-edge AI leverages robust algorithms..."
- "In today's ever-evolving lending landscape..."
- "We are pleased to offer seamless integration..."
- "FundMore is a best-in-class solution..."

### Power Themes (use as angles, not verbatim scripts)

**Compliance as infrastructure.** OSFI's B-20 requirements aren't going away. Lenders who
build AI with explainability and audit trails baked in are structurally better positioned
than those retrofitting compliance onto legacy processes.

**Decision speed as competitive moat.** Cost-to-close is a measurable line item. Every
day of manual review is a cost and a borrower experience risk. Automation turns speed into
a sustainable advantage.

**Consistency as risk reduction.** Manual underwriting introduces variance. Variance
introduces regulatory exposure and fair-lending risk. Consistent, auditable decisioning
is the risk argument for AI.

**Headless LOS as US expansion lever.** The US headless LOS doesn't ask lenders to
rip and replace their stack. It integrates into existing front-ends. The barrier to entry
for US lenders is lower because of this.

## 06. Buyer Personas

### Persona 1: CRO / Chief Risk Officer
- **Pain:** Regulatory exposure from inconsistent underwriting, audit-trail gaps, B-20
  compliance burden.
- **Outcome:** Explainable AI decisions, full audit trail, defensible process.
- **Angles:** Explainability, B-20 alignment, OSFI audit readiness.

### Persona 2: CIO / CTO
- **Pain:** Legacy origination systems, integration complexity, build vs. buy decisions.
- **Outcome:** Modern AI infrastructure that integrates without a rip-and-replace.
- **Angles:** Headless architecture, API-first, infrastructure investment rationale.

### Persona 3: Lending Executive (VP Lending / Head of Originations)
- **Pain:** High cost-to-close, slow decision cycles, losing deals to faster competitors.
- **Outcome:** Faster decisions, lower per-loan cost, better borrower experience.
- **Angles:** Decision speed, cost-to-close, competitive differentiation.

## 08. Content Build Scaffolding

**Thought-leadership post (LinkedIn):**
1. Open with the buyer's problem, in their language.
2. Name the stakes (compliance risk, cost, competitive pressure).
3. FundMore's answer in one sentence with one proof point.
4. Close with a genuine question that invites comment.
Maximum 1,300 characters. Outcome-first, never feature-first.

**Regulation / market post:**
1. State what changed and the source (Tier 1 or Tier 2 only).
2. Explain what it means for lenders in plain terms.
3. Connect to FundMore's compliance or infrastructure pillar without overclaiming.
4. Credit the source visibly.

**Product spotlight:**
1. Name the buyer and their problem.
2. State the outcome the product delivers.
3. One specific, confirmed proof point.
4. CTA: learn more or book a demo.

## 09. Brand Do's and Don'ts

### Do's
- Write "FundMore" exactly, every time.
- Lead with the customer outcome.
- Source every number; cite inline.
- Use regulatory terms (B-20, OSFI, FINTRAC) accurately and only when they apply.
- Use FundMore Blue (`#0A2463`) and Green (`#3DDC97`) in all visual assets.
- Use Raleway for headings in cards and HTML assets.
- Embed the logo from companies/fundmore/assets/ as base64 in card HTML.
- Include a visible source credit line on every card.

### Don'ts
- Never write FundMore.ai, Fundmore, Fund More, or FUNDMORE.
- Never use unconfirmed product names (e.g., "Mira") as fact.
- Never publish the unconfirmed processed-volume figure.
- Never use em dashes.
- Never open with a feature name before naming the buyer's outcome.
- Never use AI tell phrases (see core/marketing-core.md).
- Never overclaim on regulatory compliance ("FundMore makes you compliant" is wrong;
  "FundMore is designed for B-20 compliance environments" is correct).
