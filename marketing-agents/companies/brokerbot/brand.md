## 04. Voice and Tone

### Core Voice Description
BrokerBot's voice is the confident friend who happens to know your industry cold. Punchy, numbers-driven, loss-aversion framing. We speak to practitioners, not C-suites.

### Voice Spectrum
- Formal vs. Casual: leans **casual** (25% formal)
- Corporate vs. Practitioner: leans **practitioner** (70%)
- Feature-led vs. Outcome-led: leans **outcome-led** (80%)
- Soft sell vs. Loss aversion: leans **loss aversion** (75%)
- Verbose vs. Punchy: leans **punchy** (85%)
- Generic vs. Canada-specific: strongly **Canada-specific** (90%)

### Voice Pillars
**Numbers-first.** Lead with data. "$2.3M evaporated" hits harder than "you could lose revenue." Always quantify the pain.

**Direct and declarative.** Short sentences. Active voice. "BrokerBot fixes that. Automatically." No hedging.

**Practitioner-level.** Use industry terms: "book of business," "renewal," "refi," "HELOC," "Filogix," "B-20," "PIPEDA," "CASL," "5-year term." (See glossary in `references/canadian-context.md`.)

**Canadian identity.** Reference PIPEDA, B-20, Filogix, 5-year renewal cycles, CASL. Not a US product with a maple leaf sticker.

### Tone by Context

| Channel | Tone |
|---|---|
| Website / Ads | Maximum punch. Loss aversion. "70% of past clients don't return." |
| Email / Nurture | Warm but urgent. "Your clients are checking home values right now." |
| Sales / Demo | Confident, proof-heavy. "29% retention vs. 20% average." |
| Support / Onboarding | Patient, clear, encouraging. "Upload your list. BrokerBot does the rest." |
| Enterprise / Lender | Slightly more formal, ROI-centric. "Reduce borrower attrition with automated equity intelligence." |

### Write Like This
- "70% of past clients don't return. BrokerBot fixes that."
- "At $5,784 per loan, losing 400 deals = $2.3M evaporated."
- "You're not a marketer. You're a dealmaker."
- "Clients check property values like stocks."
- "Your brand, your logo, your face. Not ours."

### Never Write Like This
- "We are pleased to offer an innovative solution..."
- "Leverage our cutting-edge AI capabilities..."
- "BrokerBot is a best-in-class platform..."
- "Utilize our robust engagement engine..."
- "Our proprietary algorithms maximize ROI..."

## 06. Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

### ICP 1: Mortgage Brokers (Primary)
- **Tagline:** "Win back past clients and referrals."
- **Who:** Independent and brokerage-affiliated brokers with 100+ past clients
- **Pain:** 70% client attrition. Banks call at renewal. No time for marketing.
- **Trigger:** Renewal season pressure, lost client to bank, deals walking out the door
- **Outcome:** Automated retention that feels personal, surfaced renewal/refi opportunities, referrals
- **Decision criteria:** ROI speed (break-even at 4 clients), Canadian data accuracy, white-label, set-and-forget
- **Copy angles:** Loss aversion, identity ("dealmaker not marketer"), revenue math, referral trust, automation ease

### ICP 2: Lenders
- **Tagline:** "Retain more borrowers. Renew more loans."
- **Who:** Credit unions, monoline lenders, alternative lenders, national banks
- **Pain:** Borrower attrition at renewal, losing to broker channel, no post-funding engagement
- **Trigger:** Renewal portfolio pressure, competitive rates, board mandate for retention
- **Outcome:** Automated borrower retention at scale, reduced attrition, data-driven renewal pipeline
- **Decision criteria:** Enterprise scalability, PIPEDA/CASL compliance, integrations, white-label
- **Copy angles:** Scale, ROI, compliance as a feature, data depth

### ICP 3: Real Estate Agents
- **Tagline:** "Stay their agent long after possession day."
- **Who:** Listing agents, buyer agents, teams with past-client databases
- **Pain:** Clients forget you post-possession. Portals capture the next listing.
- **Trigger:** Lost listing to a portal lead, past clients listed with someone else
- **Outcome:** Stay top-of-mind between transactions, surface move-up candidates
- **Decision criteria:** Ease of setup, content value, co-branding with mortgage partners
- **Copy angles:** Relevance ("forget names, not value"), no awkward check-ins, listing pipeline

### ICP 4: Appraisers
- **Tagline:** "Stop being a commodity. Be the market intelligence expert."
- **Who:** Independent appraisers and appraisal firms
- **Pain:** Commoditized service, no engagement between appraisals
- **Trigger:** Differentiate from AMC competition, build referral relationships
- **Outcome:** Position as market-knowledge authority
- **Decision criteria:** Data accuracy, professional presentation, ease of use
- **Copy angles:** Authority, differentiation, referral network building

### ICP 5: Insurance Professionals
- **Tagline:** "Every equity milestone is an insurance conversation."
- **Who:** P&C brokers, home insurance specialists
- **Pain:** Passive renewals, missing cross-sell moments
- **Trigger:** Increase retention, cross-sell into mortgage referral networks
- **Outcome:** Surface triggers, cross-sell at milestones
- **Decision criteria:** Workflow integration, trigger accuracy
- **Copy angles:** Milestones, cross-sell, broker-agent ecosystem

## 07. Competitive Positioning

### Primary Competitors
- **Ownwell** (Canadian): Claims "only Canadian-owned retention platform," 7X ROI, 66% open rates.
- **HomeBot** (US): Claims 8M+ homeowners, 50% engagement, predictive scoring, mobile apps.

### Capability Comparison

| Capability | BrokerBot | Ownwell | HomeBot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canadian Property Data | 18M+ via OPTA/FCT | Third-party provider | US data only |
| White-Label Branding | Full white-label | Partial (from-name only) | Co-branded |
| Verticals Served | 5 verticals | 2 (Broker + Agent) | 5+ verticals |
| AI Lead Pre-Qualification | Built-in | Not available | Behavioral signals only |
| Renewal Tracking | Built-in | Opportunity surfacing | N/A (US 30-year) |
| Bilingual (EN/FR) | Yes | English only | English only |
| PIPEDA Compliance | Yes | Yes | US compliance only |
| 5-Year Renewal Cycle | Designed for it | Designed for it | 30-year US model |
| Diagnostic Tools | Ghost Score Calculator | Not available | Likelihood to Sell |
| Enterprise Reach | Individual to national bank | Brokerage-level | Banks, CUs, enterprise |

### Battlecard vs. Ownwell
- **Win on:** Proprietary 18M database, full white-label, 5 verticals vs. 2, AI pre-qualification, renewal tracking, bilingual.
- **They claim:** "Only Canadian-owned retention platform," 7X ROI, 66% open rates.
- **Counter:** Data advantage is unmatched. 18M OPTA/FCT records vs. a third-party feed. Five verticals, not two.

### Battlecard vs. HomeBot
- **Win on:** Canadian coverage (they have zero), 5-year renewal design, PIPEDA, bilingual, OPTA/FCT data.
- **They claim:** 8M+ homeowners, 50% engagement, predictive scoring, mobile apps.
- **Counter:** Great US product, irrelevant in Canada. No Canadian data, compliance, or 5-year understanding.

**Public rule:** Never name HomeBot in public-facing content. Say "US platforms."

## 09. Presentation and Slide Standards

### Slide Strategy
- Default background: white (`#FFFFFF`) with `#2F3439` text.
- Reserve dark (`#0B1818`) for: title, key stat reveals, and CTA.
- Rhythm: content on light, impact on dark.

### Deck Length Variants

**10-slide standard sales deck:**
1. Title (dark) 2. The Problem 3. Revenue Math 4. Why It Happens 5. How BrokerBot Fixes It
6. Features (3 max) 7. Data Advantage 8. Social Proof 9. Pricing / ROI 10. CTA (dark)

**5-slide short deck (pitch / follow-up):**
1. Title (dark) 2. The Problem (one stat) 3. Revenue Math 4. The Fix (3 features max) 5. CTA (dark)

**3-slide micro deck (teaser / cold outreach):**
1. The Problem stat (dark) 2. The Fix + one proof stat (light) 3. CTA (dark)

### Slide Design Rules
- Headlines: Gothic A1 Black 900, under 10 words, pink for stats
- Body: Mulish Regular, max 4 lines per slide
- One key number per slide; if two, split the slide
- Charts: pink for BrokerBot, slate (`#34495E`) for competitors
- Problem first on a dark slide; lead with loss
- End with proof in the final third
- CTA slide (dark): always "Book a Demo," never "Questions?"

## 10. Content Build Scaffolding

Reusable skeletons for net-new pieces. Fill each with the right ICP's pain, a key stat, and the correct CTA. Always pull live snippets from `references/copy-bank.md`.

**Ad (LinkedIn / social):**
1. Loss-aversion or revenue-math hook (one or two lines)
2. The uncomfortable truth ("Most brokers know they should... Most don't.")
3. What BrokerBot does, white-label, in one sentence
4. One proof stat (29% vs. 20%, break-even at 4)
5. Direct CTA ("Book a demo and stop the leak.")

**Nurture email:**
1. Subject: a question or a number, never a greeting
2. Open on a specific, relatable client scenario
3. Name the silent cost
4. The fix in one sentence, branded to them
5. Soft but clear CTA; warm-but-urgent tone

**Landing page:**
1. Hero: loss-aversion headline + dark background + pink CTA
2. Revenue math section (the numbers)
3. How it works (3 features max)
4. Data advantage (18M+, OPTA/FCT, Canadian)
5. Social proof / case study
6. Pricing or ROI
7. Final CTA on dark

**Always:** lead with pain, quantify it, name the white-label benefit, close with a demo CTA.

## 11. Brand Do's and Don'ts

### Do's
- Default to white/light backgrounds; use dark (`#0B1818`) strategically for hero/stat/CTA blocks
- Use pink (`#FC0A7E`) for CTAs/highlights; test purple (`#86308B`) on pricing
- Lead with pain/loss aversion, then pivot to the solution
- Quantify everything: $5,784, 29%, 18M+, 82%
- Speak to practitioners; reference PIPEDA, B-20, 5-year terms, Filogix
- Use Gothic A1 for headings, Mulish for body (Google Fonts only)
- Include "re engage" with the logo where space permits
- Button specs: 50px height, 3px radius, 20px min padding, always

### Don'ts
- Never default to all-dark page designs
- Never use buzzwords: "leverage," "synergize," "cutting-edge," "robust," "best-in-class"
- **Never use em dashes.** Use commas, colons, semicolons, or rewrite instead.
- Never put BrokerBot's brand ahead of the customer's (white-label first)
- Never use stock photos of handshakes or generic office scenes
- Never name HomeBot publicly (say "US platforms")
- Never promise specific ROI without citing a published case study
- Never use passive voice in headlines
- Never substitute non-Google fonts for Gothic A1 or Mulish
- Never modify the logo colours outside purple (`#86308B`) and white

### Logo Usage
- **Logo_light** (purple icon + dark text): light backgrounds
- **Logo_dark** (white icon + white text): dark/coloured backgrounds
- **Logo_small:** nav bars, tight spaces, app icons
- Export: PNG @2x and SVG. Favicon: ICO at 64x64px
- Min 120px wide digital, 1 inch print. Maintain aspect ratio.
- Never on busy backgrounds without a solid overlay; never rotate, skew, or add effects
- Never change the icon colour from purple or white; never separate icon from wordmark formally

## 12. Pre-Flight Checklist (run before delivering ANY output)

Before returning copy, a deck, or a design, confirm:

1. **Em dashes:** zero. Scan for them. Replace with commas, colons, semicolons, or a rewrite.
2. **Buzzwords:** zero. No leverage, synergize, cutting-edge, robust, best-in-class.
3. **Stat-led:** at least one hard number appears, ideally in the opening.
4. **Active voice** in every headline.
5. **Loss aversion** framing leads, solution follows.
6. **Canadian specificity:** at least one of PIPEDA, CASL, B-20, OPTA/FCT, 5-year term, bilingual where relevant.
7. **White-label respected:** the customer's brand is the face, never BrokerBot's.
8. **Correct CTA:** "Book a Demo" energy, never "Questions?" or "Learn more" as the primary.
9. **Tone matches channel** (see Section 04 table).
10. **Fonts/colours** correct if visual: Gothic A1 + Mulish, pink CTA, white default, dark accents only.
11. **Numbers verified**, especially the 82% double-use flag (Section 05).
12. **Competitors:** HomeBot not named publicly.

If any check fails, fix it before delivering. Do not ship a near-miss.
